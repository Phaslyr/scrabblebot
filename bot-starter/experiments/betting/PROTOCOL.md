# Bid prediction experiment

This experiment is offline and does not change or import an online entry point. Existing bot policies, settings, saved models, and launchers stay unchanged.

1. Fetch anonymous public completed-match records for the last 1,800 games. Audit cash, score, and rack-size ledgers. Choose five established active leaders with enough current-version games in each chronological partition.
2. Split whole games globally into 60% fitting, 20% calibration, and 20% final test. Use opening-state public features only. Winning bids are exact when archived; Vickrey losses give an upper bound at the runner-up payment. A reserve payment of one only identifies the maximum losing bid as between zero and one. Late revisions exceeding opening cash remain part of test errors.
3. Compare pooled letter/balance medians and a letter/phase/public-cash market table with identity-aware letter medians and identity-aware censored feature models. Choose each identity's family and the stronger simple baseline on calibration games. Calibrate a central 80% maximum-rival-bid interval on calibration games. Primary market evaluation requires six known real players. Score final-test maximum-rival forecasts by game, not by pretending correlated auctions are independent.
4. Compare unchanged Wags/TaylorMason with (a) simple pooled forecasts, (b) identity-aware forecasts, and (c) an extra-aggression control with the same maximum bid extension. Forecast responses may extend a valuable bid by at most four coins under a hard cash/value ceiling and wait for a low-cost completion when the forecast supports it. They never bid just above a forecast without a value ceiling.
5. Use identical new bags, seats and tie randomness for every policy. Main opponents independently resample held-out public bid intervals, with midpoint/lower/upper assumptions for hidden losing bids; report those assumptions. A separate cached-model arena is a sensitivity check, not sole evidence. Shared first places count as wins.
6. Predictive evidence is promising if identity-aware maximum-rival error falls at least 10% versus the calibration-selected simple baseline with at least 75% coverage for the intended 80% interval. Gameplay evidence requires improvement over unchanged policy and evidence beyond the simple-forecast/extra-aggression controls. Report uncertainty and mixed results. Do not deploy automatically even if these checks pass.

The empirical arenas approximate private word choice and counterfactual behavior. Their results cannot establish public-server win rates. Individual losing bids remain unidentified; they are never represented as observed zeros.
